    Module 00

    Prerequisites & Studio Setup

    Your gear, Procreate's interface, and how to plan a canvas that prints sharply.

    Lesson 0.1

    Pixels: what you are painting with

    Procreate paints with pixels, a fixed grid of coloured squares. Zoom in far enough and every stroke breaks into blocks.

    Raster (Procreate)

    Great for textures, soft blends and painterly looks. Can't be enlarged without going soft.

    Vector (Illustrator, Affinity)

    Made of maths, so it stays sharp at any size. Best for logos. Many pros letter in Procreate, then trace to vector.

    Module 02

    Drawing Fundamentals for the Screen

    Confident lines, simple forms, perspective, light and proportion. These are the skills behind every good illustration.

    Lesson 2.1

    Confident lines

    Draw one fast stroke from the shoulder, not many small hairy ones from the wrist.

    1Mark a start dot and an end dot
    2Ghost it: move your hand over the path 2–3 times without touching the glass
    3Draw it in one fast stroke. Undo and repeat rather than patching

    Lesson 2.2

    Shapes → forms

    Everything you draw is built from four 3D forms. Learn them, and you can draw anything.

    Example: a coffee cup is a cylinder plus a torus

    Lesson 2.3

    Gesture & silhouette

    Start a figure with one line of action. Check readability by filling it black.

    Silhouette test: can you tell what it is?

    ✕ Arms hidden. It reads as a blob.

    ✓ Limbs separated. "Waving a flag" reads instantly.

    Lesson 2.4

    Perspective

    Parallel lines meet at vanishing points on the horizon, which is at your eye level.

    1-point

    Facing a road, corridor or room head-on

    2-point

    Looking at a corner: buildings, furniture

    3-point

    Dramatic views looking up or down

    In ProcreateActions → Canvas → Drawing Guide → Edit Drawing Guide → Perspective. Tap the canvas to place 1, 2 or 3 vanishing points. Turn on Drawing Assist on a layer and your strokes snap to the perspective lines.

    Lesson 2.5

    Light & shadow

    Every lit object has the same six zones. Drag the sun around and watch them move.

    Shadow on a clipped Multiply layer, highlight on a clipped Add layer (Module 01).

    Lesson 2.6

    Value: the hidden structure

    Value means light vs dark. If a piece doesn't read in greyscale, colour won't save it.

    1 light
    2
    3 mid
    4
    5 dark

    Greyscale check: drag to see the values

    ✕ All mid-values. Everything merges in grey.

    ✓ Dark / light / mid. Reads clearly.

    In ProcreateAdd a top layer filled with black, set it to Saturation (or Color), and toggle it on and off to check your values.

    Lesson 2.7

    Proportion shortcuts

    Measure with the head: an adult is about 7½–8 heads tall, and the eyes sit halfway down the head.

    These are starting points, not rules. Stylised characters deliberately break them, for example with big heads that read as "cute".

    Lesson 2.8

    Symmetry & Drawing Assist

    Draw one side and Procreate mirrors it. Use it for logos, mandalas, ornaments and faces.

    Vertical
    Horizontal
    Quadrant
    Radial
    Actions›Canvas›Drawing Guide on›Edit Drawing Guide›Symmetry

    Module 03

    Colour Theory & Colour Combination Types

    How colours are described, how to combine them, and how to make palettes that set a mood.

    Lesson 3.1

    Hue, saturation, brightness

    Every colour has three properties. Procreate's Classic panel uses the same three sliders.

    Hue

    Which colour family

    Saturation

    Grey → pure

    Brightness (value)

    Dark → light

    Notice that pure yellow is very light in grey and pure blue is very dark, even at the same brightness setting.

    Lesson 3.2

    The colour wheel

    Twelve hues, 30° apart. Every colour combination type is a shape drawn on this wheel.

    Screen wheel (RGB)

    This is what Procreate's colour disc uses. Primaries are red, green and blue. Blue's opposite is yellow.

    Painter's wheel (RYB)

    Traditional paint theory. Primaries are red, yellow and blue. Blue's opposite is orange. You'll see it in older art books.

    Lesson 3.3

    Colour combination types

    Pick a type, spin the wheel and watch the poster recolour.

    The seven types, same poster

    In ProcreateOpen the Colour panel and choose Harmony. Tap the word under the title to switch between Complementary, Split Complementary, Analogous, Triadic and Tetradic.

    Lesson 3.4

    Warm & cool

    Warm colours come forward and cool colours recede. Use warm light with cool shadows, or the reverse.

    warm
    cool

    Purple shadows next to orange light look more alive than brown shadows.

    Lesson 3.5

    60-30-10 & contrast

    Don't use your colours in equal amounts. Use 60% dominant, 30% secondary and 10% accent.

    Four kinds of contrast that create a focal point

    Value

    Light on dark

    Saturation

    Vivid among greys

    Temperature

    Warm in cool

    Complementary

    Opposites vibrate

    Lesson 3.6

    Mood & context

    The same composition can tell six different stories depending on its palette.

    Context changes colour: both centre squares are the same grey

    Always judge a colour in its surroundings. Use the eyedropper rather than trusting your eyes.

    Lesson 3.7

    Palettes in Procreate

    The five views of the Colour panel, and how to pull a palette from a photo.

    Disc

    Quick, intuitive picking

    Classic

    HSB square + sliders

    Harmony

    Combination types

    Value

    Exact numbers & hex

    Palettes

    Saved swatch sets

    Palette from a photo

    Lesson 3.8

    Gradient maps & grading

    Paint in grey once, then try out whole colour schemes in seconds with a Gradient Map.

    Adjustments›Gradient Map›pick a preset or tap + for your own
    GradingFor subtle unity, add a top layer filled with one colour and set it to Soft Light or Color at 10–25%.

    Lesson 4.1

    The pipeline

    Every finished illustration goes through the same stages. Each one goes on its own layer.

    Matching layer stack

    2. LightingAdd · clipped
    3. Line artReference
    4. ShadowsMultiply · clipped

    Lesson 4.2

    Thumbnails

    Before committing, try 6–9 tiny compositions using only three greys. Pick the strongest one.

    The highlighted thumbnail wins: a strong dark/light split, a clear focal glow, and a sun that balances the house.

    Tiny canvas

    About 600 × 800 px, so detail is impossible

    3 greys only

    Light, mid and dark

    Speed beats polish here

    Lesson 4.3

    Sketch → refined sketch

    Rough it loose. Then lower that layer's opacity and trace a cleaner version on a new layer.

    1Sketch layer → opacity 25–35%
    2Alpha Lock → fill light blue, so it won't be confused with ink
    3Use Liquify or Transform to fix proportions now, not later

    Lesson 4.4

    Line weight

    Use thick lines for outer edges and thin lines for inner details. This one change adds depth instantly.

    Silhouette

    Thickest

    Overlaps

    Thicker where one form sits in front of another

    Details

    Thinnest

    Light side

    Thin on the lit side, thick in shadow

    Lesson 4.5

    Flats

    Fill every object with one flat colour, on its own layer, beneath the line art.

    Line art → Reference›New layer below›ColorDrop each area›One layer per object
    TipIf ColorDrop leaves a thin halo along the lines, raise the ColorDrop threshold, or fill the area slightly bigger so it tucks under the line art.

    Lesson 4.6

    Rendering styles

    Same drawing, four finishes. Pick the one that suits the project.

    Brushes

    Hard Airbrush, Monoline

    Brushes

    Studio Pen + Hard Airbrush on Multiply

    Brushes

    Soft Brush, Nikko Rull, Smudge

    Brushes

    Halftone / grain brushes, Noise adjustment

    Lesson 4.7

    Light & atmosphere

    Far things get lighter, bluer and lower in contrast. Light sources glow.

    Night version: a navy layer on Multiply over everything, then a soft yellow glow on Screen/Add at the window.

    Lesson 4.8

    Finishing & checks

    Fresh eyes catch mistakes. Flip the canvas and check it in greyscale before calling it done.

    Flip: Actions → Canvas → Flip canvas horizontally. The tilt jumps out.

    Value check: is the focal point the highest-contrast area?

    ✓Flip horizontally
    ✓Greyscale check
    ✓Zoom out to thumbnail size: does it still read?
    ✓Texture layer on Overlay at 20–35%
    ✓Clean the edges around the focal point
    ✓Export .procreate + PNG/JPEG

    Lesson 4.9 · Project

    Project: your own pipeline

    Pick a small subject (a house, mug, plant or pet) and take it through all six stages, then into two styles.

    1Draw 6 thumbnails (3 greys, 2 minutes each), then pick one
    2Rough sketch → refined sketch (blue, 30%)
    3Line art with varied line weight, set as Reference
    4Flats, one layer per object
    5Shadows (Multiply) + light (Add), clipped
    6Texture + checks, then duplicate the artwork and restyle it as flat or riso

    Lesson 5.1

    Visual hierarchy

    Decide what people should see first, second and third, then make it bigger, bolder or brighter in that order.

    2

    3

    Lesson 5.2

    Gestalt principles

    Your brain groups things automatically. Designers use that to organise without adding extra lines.

    Proximity

    Close things belong together: two groups

    Similarity

    Same colour or shape reads as a row

    Closure

    You "see" a triangle that isn't drawn

    Continuity

    Eyes follow lines and curves

    Figure / ground

    Space between one pair of letters

    WORD

    Tracking

    Space across a whole word or line

    line one
    line two

    Leading

    Space between lines

    Module 07

    Text Art, Hand Lettering & Calligraphy

    Drawing letters instead of typing them: brush script, faux calligraphy, built-up letters and dimensional effects.

    Lesson 7.1

    Lettering vs calligraphy vs type

    Calligraphy

    Written in one stroke per part, with thick and thin from pressure

    Lettering

    Drawn and built up: outlined, filled and decorated

    Typography

    Arranging pre-made fonts

    Lesson 7.2

    Guidelines

    Consistent letters need guides: four horizontal lines plus slant lines.

    1Draw the guide sheet once and save it as a PNG
    2Actions → Add → Insert a file, then lock the layer and lower its opacity
    3Or use Drawing Guide → 2D Grid for the horizontals

    Lesson 7.3

    The basic strokes

    Every script letter is built from these eight strokes. Drill them before you write words.

    The one ruleThin going up, thick coming down. Press on downstrokes and float on upstrokes. Write slowly: calligraphy is drawing, not handwriting.

    Lesson 7.4

    Brush lettering pad

    Practise here. This pad imitates a brush pen by reading the direction of your stroke.

    Procreate brushes

    Calligraphy set → Script or Brush Pen, or Inking → Studio Pen

    StreamLine

    60–90% for silky curves

    Pressure curve

    Sag it slightly so upstrokes stay thin

    Lesson 7.5

    Faux calligraphy

    No pressure control? Write it in monoline, then draw the thick downstrokes in by hand.

    Lesson 7.6

    Building letterforms

    For bold display lettering, build the word in layers: skeleton → weight → details.

    ✓Weight goes on the downstrokes, even in block letters
    ✓Draw each step on a new layer and lower the opacity of the layer below

    Lesson 7.7

    Lettering styles

    Monoline scriptcasual, quick
    Brush scriptthick/thin, lively
    Serif capselegant, classic
    Bold blockloud, posters
    3D blockretro, signage
    Outlinelight, modern
    Bannerbadges, vintage
    Archedemblems, labels

    Lesson 7.8

    Dimensional effects

    Each effect is just a few duplicated layers.

    Drop shadow

    Duplicate the text → move it down and right → fill it dark → Multiply, 30–60%

    3D extrude

    Duplicate many times, nudging each copy 1–2 px diagonally, then merge the copies and darken them

    Neon glow

    Glow sandwich: blurred copies on Add beneath the letters (Lesson 1.10)

    Retro chrome

    Clipping Mask layer with a hard-edged gradient: light sky above, dark horizon below

    Outline

    Text tool → Edit Style → Outline, or a thick stroke on a layer below

    Texture

    Clipped layer: erase with a grainy brush, or a texture on Overlay

    Lesson 7.9

    Composing words

    Fit words into a shape. Mix styles: one star word plus supporting words.

    In ProcreateTo curve hand-lettered words, rasterise them, then use Transform → Warp (or Advanced Mesh) and drag the mesh into an arch.

    Lesson 8.8

    Print preparation

    Each A size is half the one above it. Same proportions, so artwork scales cleanly between them.

    ✓Size + bleed: A3 297 × 420 mm → canvas 303 × 426 mm at 300 DPI (Lesson 5.7)
    ✓CMYK canvas from the start, or proof RGB colours carefully (Lesson 0.7)
    ✓Text inside the safe zone, nothing important near the trim
    ✓Black text: use pure K black for small text, rich black for big areas (ask your printer)
    ✓Export: Share → PDF (Best quality) or TIFF, plus a .procreate backup
    ✓Test print at home, scaled down, and check it at arm's length

    Lesson 9.1

    Make a brush shape

    A brush shape is just a black-and-white image. White means paint.

    TipGrain works the same way: Grain → Edit → Import. Use a seamless texture (paper, canvas, concrete) so no seams show as the stroke repeats.

    Lesson 9.3

    Combine, share & back up brushes

    Combine

    Brush Library: tap brush A, swipe right on brush B → Combine. Each half keeps its own settings.

    Share

    Swipe left on a brush → Share (.brush), or tap a set name → Share (.brushset)

    Import

    Tap a .brush or .brushset file in Files, or AirDrop it. It lands in the Imported set.

    Lesson 9.4

    Animation Assist

    Each layer becomes a frame. Onion skin shows the neighbouring frames faintly so you can draw the in-betweens.

    Actions›Canvas›Animation Assist›Add Frame›Play
    Loop

    Repeats forever: walk cycles, GIFs

    Ping-Pong

    Plays forward, then backward: breathing, floating

    One Shot

    Plays once: reveals, intros

    Onion skin

    Up to 12 frames, with adjustable opacity

    TipTap a frame in the timeline → Background to keep scenery fixed behind every frame, or set Hold duration to pause on a key pose without redrawing it.

    Lesson 9.5

    Animation principles

    Three principles make the biggest difference to small loops.

    Squash & stretch

    Stretch when moving fast, squash on impact. Keep the volume the same.

    Ease in / ease out

    Bunch the frames near the start and end of a move

    Anticipation

    Crouch before the jump. Move a little the opposite way first.

    Lesson 9.6

    Power features

    Hidden tools that professionals use every day.

    QuickMenu

    A radial menu of six favourite actions. Assign it in Gesture controls.

    Recolor

    Adjustments → Recolor: drag the crosshair onto a colour to swap it across the layer

    Reference window

    Actions → Canvas → Reference: a floating image or zoomed-out view of your canvas

    Liquify

    Push, twirl, pinch and expand pixels. Fixes proportions and makes fluid type.

    Symmetry + Assist

    Draw half a logo or ornament, get the whole (Lesson 2.8)

    3-finger copy & paste

    Swipe down for Cut / Copy / Duplicate / Paste

    3D painting

    Import an OBJ or USDZ model and paint on it, with lighting and AR view

    Time-lapse

    Actions → Video → Time-lapse Replay / Export. Free content for social media.

    Module 10

    Export, Print & Portfolio

    Get your work out of the iPad: the right file for each destination, prints and merch, a portfolio, and pricing.

    Lesson 10.1

    Which file format?

    Ask one question: where is it going?

    JPEG compression smears flat colours and sharp edges. Use PNG for illustration, lettering and anything with text.

    Lesson 10.2

    Exporting for social media

    Respect the app's interface zones, and split wide art into carousels.

    Carousel: one wide artwork split into 3 slides

    Canvas 3240 × 1350 → Crop & Resize or select thirds → export each 1080 × 1350.

    ✓Export in sRGB, at 1080 px wide or 2×
    ✓PNG for crisp art. Apps re-compress, so start from the best quality
    ✓Post a time-lapse or a process carousel alongside the final piece

    Lesson 10.3

    Printing your art

    Screens glow and paper reflects, so expect prints to be slightly darker and duller. Proof before printing a big batch.

    Matte

    No glare. Illustration and riso looks.

    Glossy / satin

    Vivid colour. Photos and saturated art.

    Fine-art textured

    Giclée prints for selling originals

    1Order one test print first
    2Too dark? Duplicate the artwork and lift the shadows with Curves for a "print version"
    3Lower your screen brightness when you judge colours, because a bright screen fools you
    4Send a PDF or TIFF at final size + bleed (Lesson 8.8)
